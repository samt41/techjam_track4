==== SLIDE 1 AI-MEMORY TARGETTED PRODUCT SEARCH ====

Hi everyone. We are OpenCheliped: Samuel, Cervon, and Weichu. This is AI-Memory Targetted Product Search, a shopping agent that remembers what the customer wants as the conversation changes.

==== SLIDE 2 SEARCH THAT REMEMBERS. ====

The default starter sends the latest customer message into BM25 as a one-shot lexical query. We turn the full conversation into structured intent, search an indexed catalog, enforce the customer's constraints, and return a ranked product slate we can explain. The system searches 50,000 products locally on CPU with zero online API calls, model tokens, GPU requirements, or credentials.

==== SLIDE 3 LARGELY IMPROVED OVER BM25. ====

The architecture has five main steps. First, the Constraint Extractor converts each message into typed facts. Second, the Preference Ledger updates the active intent. Third, the Search Planner runs structured and full-text retrieval routes. Fourth, the Eligibility Gate removes products that violate hard requirements. Finally, the Candidate Belief Model ranks the survivors and the Question Policy chooses the most useful clarification. BM25 remains one retrieval signal, but it is no longer the entire decision process.

==== SLIDE 4 THE END OF FORGETFUL SEARCH. ====

A shopper might ask for boots, add an 80-dollar budget, exclude leather, and then switch to hiking shoes. To a person, that is one request evolving over time. The starter sees four separate BM25 queries. It forgets the budget, cannot reliably represent the exclusion, and does not know the final category replaces the first one. On the public set, the starter reaches 0.125 Hit Rate at 10, 0.068034 MRR, and 9.81 mean turns to first correct.

==== SLIDE 5 OUR RESULTS ====

The default SQLite FTS5 BM25 starter scores 0.10671 TechnicalScore. Our deterministic, memory-backed agent scores 0.76884 on the same 200-session public evaluator. That is a 7.21-times improvement in the organizer's combined score.

==== SLIDE 6 OUR AGENT SEEKS MEANING. ====

We store meaning as typed constraints, not as one long search string. Each constraint records its attribute, value, strength, exclusion, confidence, and operation. Required leather, preferred leather, and not leather therefore produce different state changes.

If the customer says, "I prefer red boots," and then, "Ignore that; I need leather boots," the ledger removes red, preserves boots, adds leather, and increments the intent version. SET, REMOVE, DECLINE, and RETRACT PROVISIONAL operations make corrections and "no preference" replies explicit. BM25 can match the word leather; our ledger understands what the customer meant by it.

==== SLIDE 7 1 SEARCH ROUTE'S NOT ENOUGH. ====

Structured metadata finds exact attributes, while SQLite FTS5 finds relevant language. We combine the routes with Reciprocal Rank Fusion at k equals 60, cap each route at 1,000 hits, and materialize at most 5,000 candidates.

A deterministic eligibility gate then removes anything that violates a hard requirement or explicit exclusion. The remaining products are ranked with Bayesian evidence from retrieval routes, soft preferences, and privacy-safe aggregate profiles. Exact ties break on parent_asin, so repeated runs stay deterministic. This improves on the starter's single OR-based BM25 order without adding an online dependency.

==== SLIDE 8 A 7.36X LEAP OVER THE STARTER. ====

On the unchanged 200-session public evaluator, the starter finds 25 targets and our agent finds 184. Hit Rate at 10 rises from 12.5 percent to 92 percent: 159 additional successful sessions and a 7.36-times result.

MRR rises from 0.068034 to 0.524466, a 670.89 percent improvement. Mean turns to first correct falls from 9.81 to 3.425, a 65.09 percent reduction. TechnicalScore rises from 0.10671 to 0.76884, a 620.49 percent improvement. These are public-set results; the private evaluation remains the generalization test.

==== SLIDE 9 THE FIXES THAT MOVED THE NEEDLE. ====

The largest gains came from catalog structure and conversation state, not a larger model. Attribute classification, catalog-derived material recovery, and better override retention moved Hit Rate at 10 from 0.760 to 0.915. Separator normalization moved it again to 0.920.

We found inconsistent colon spacing in 131 concepts across 705 products. Normalizing those concepts moved one target from rank 154 to rank one. We also replaced correlated SQL filters taking 263 to 293 milliseconds with posting-set filters taking 3 to 7 milliseconds. Intent Override improved from 0.20 to 0.90.

==== SLIDE 10 EXPERIMENTS WE REFUSED TO OVERSELL. ====

We measured every idea on paired sessions before shipping it. The statistical gate uses a paired bootstrap, paired permutation test, Holm-Bonferroni correction, minimum detectable difference, and a winner's-curse correction over 10,000 replicates.

Always-on tail exploration and a popularity tie-break changed zero outcomes. The forced TF-IDF fallback showed a TechnicalScore increase of 0.006110, but its 95 percent confidence interval crossed zero and its Holm-adjusted p-value was 1.0, so the result was not detectable. Improving on BM25 meant keeping reproducible gains and rejecting extra complexity that did not measure up.

==== SLIDE 11 TEST THE CONVERSATION, NOT JUST THE FUNCTION. ====

Our 756 automated tests cover memory, overrides, exclusions, declined questions, deterministic ranking, evaluator integrity, and the organizer contract. We verify that "red boots" followed by "ignore that; leather boots" removes red but preserves boots, and that "not leather" is never relaxed even when the strict result pool is empty. The tests prove state transitions, not only lexical retrieval.

==== SLIDE 13 DEMO - INTENT CHANGES, THE RANKING CHANGES. ====

This is public session 0003, an Intent Override case run through the real agent. The target is B09YMTWDXJ, a Casio AQ-800E-7A wristwatch.

The customer first asks for a stainless-steel-band watch. The target appears at rank two, but the intended override has not happened yet, so it is correctly not counted as a hit. On turn two, the target leaves the top ten.

On turn three, the customer says, "Actually, ignore my earlier preference. What I need is: Water Resistant." The ledger retracts the stale preference, preserves the compatible watch context, and reranks the same offline catalog. The target returns at rank one. Final result: hit on turn three at rank one.
