    `HOW_TO_READ` is the "How to read this report" stated-assumptions block. It must
    cover exactly four points, because three numbers in this report will legitimately
    differ from figures quoted elsewhere in `.planning/`, and stating why converts
    three apparent inconsistencies into three demonstrations of statistical care:

    1. **Per-bucket sigma.** Each scenario row's binomial sigma is computed from
       that bucket's OWN observed p and n, not from the overall p. MEAS-09's
       illustrative `0.086` and `0.050` came from applying the overall `p = 0.92`
       to the bucket n; the bucket's own `p = 0.90` gives `0.094868` at n=10 and
       `0.054772` at n=30. The report prints the latter and that is correct.
    2. **Winner's-curse sigma-hat.** The sigma fed to the order-statistic correction
       is the PAIRED-DIFFERENCE bootstrap SE of TechnicalScore, typically 0.002 to
       0.008 on this data — not the 0.019 absolute binomial SE of HR@10 quoted in
       PROJECT.md and PITFALLS.md. Selection happens on the paired delta, so the
       noise in the selection statistic is the paired-delta noise. The resulting
       correction is roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the 0.022-0.030
       figure quoted elsewhere, and that is the methodologically correct number.
       Sigma-hat, k and `E[max of k]` are printed as separate columns so the
       corrected delta can be re-derived rather than trusted.
    3. **Achievable MDD.** PROJECT.md's "3,900-15,700 paired sessions to detect
       delta TS = 0.01" describes the weakly-correlated regime. Measured on this
       repository's 200 real sessions, a realistic ranking candidate that promotes
       10 sessions to rank 1 yields delta TS `+0.011931` against an MDD of roughly
       `0.0104` — detectable at n=200. Pairing is doing all the work: the paired
       bootstrap SE is `0.003715` where an effectively unpaired one is `0.025922`.
    4. **Per-scenario numbers are deliberately NOT Holm-corrected.** The Holm family
       is the non-baseline candidates against a common baseline in one adjudication
       event (D-19). Per-scenario rows are descriptive non-inferiority gates with
       stated sigma, not primary hypotheses. Folding four scenarios in would inflate
       the family fourfold, destroy power on the one comparison that decides
       anything, and add power to a Boundary bucket of n=10 that can detect nothing
       regardless. The omission is deliberate, not an oversight.

    Then a short **verdict vocabulary** paragraph defining all four values a
    `verdict` cell can hold, because a reader who guesses at them will mis-read the
    adjudication table: `win` — all three criteria passed jointly; `significant,
    below ship bar` — the difference IS statistically detected but fails the
    corrected 0.01 floor and/or the HR@10 exchange-rate check, so it is real and
    not worth shipping; `no difference` — not significant, and the test WAS powered
    to see an effect of the observed size; `not detectable` — not significant, and
    the observed delta sits below the MDD, so the null is uninformative and must
    not be read as evidence of equivalence. State that the `failed criteria` column
    names exactly which bar a non-win row missed.

    Also state, in one sentence each: that the practical floor of 0.01
    TechnicalScore is exactly two best-case session flips out of 200 (a single
