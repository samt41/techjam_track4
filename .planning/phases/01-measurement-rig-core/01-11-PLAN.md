<objective>
Close the reported-number-correctness warning in `arena/statistics.py:154-155`: the
bootstrap percentile indices are asymmetric at every resample count and silently
degenerate below roughly R=40.

Purpose: the interval printed in the committed leaderboard's `95% CI` column is
currently a 94.99% interval whose two bounds are drawn from different distances from
the tails (order statistic 251 from the bottom, 9750 from the top at R=10,000). At
`resamples=2` the "97.5th percentile" is literally the minimum replicate. `resamples`
is a public keyword argument the suite already exercises at 200 and 500, so a future
caller at a smaller R gets a silently wrong interval with no error. A rig whose
published interval does not have the coverage it claims cannot be certified as
statistically honest.

Why this is its own plan: `arena/statistics.py` is disjoint from every other file in
this gap-closure round, so this runs in wave 1 in parallel with the adjudication repair
with zero merge surface. It is also the one change in the round that moves a number
already printed in the committed report, so isolating it keeps that attribution clean
for the regeneration plan.

Output: an index computation that is symmetric and at-or-above nominal coverage by
construction, exposed as an auditable helper, plus a resample floor that fails loudly
instead of degenerating.
</objective>

    Three additions and two replacements in `arena/statistics.py`. No new import beyond

    **Add `MINIMUM_RESAMPLES = 40`** as a public module constant, placed beside
    `RESAMPLE_COUNT`. Comment the *why*: a 2.5% tail is representable only when
    `1 / (resamples + 1) <= 0.025`, i.e. only when `resamples >= 39`. Below that the
    `(resamples + 1) * 0.025` product falls under 1, both indices clamp to the extremes,
    and the function returns the full range of the replicates whatever confidence was
    requested -- a silently wrong answer rather than a wide one, which is how the pre-fix
    code produced an "upper bound" equal to the minimum replicate at R=2. `40` is the
    floor WR-04 proposes and sits one above that representability threshold. Record that
    the interval remains the full replicate range up to R=78 and that the lower bound
    first leaves index 0 at R=79 -- that is expected and honest at those counts, not a
    defect, and it is why the floor is about representability rather than about the span.
    Record too that the suite's existing `FAST_RESAMPLES=200`, `STABLE_RESAMPLES=500`,
    `TEST_RESAMPLES=500` and the pinned `2000` in `test_permutation_floor` all sit
    comfortably above it.

    **Add `_LOWER_QUANTILE = 0.025` and `_UPPER_QUANTILE = 0.975`** as private module
    constants, so the interval's confidence level appears once rather than as four
    magic numbers inside an index expression.

    **Add `percentile_indices(resamples: int) -> tuple[int, int]`** — a public, pure,
    side-effect-free function returning the zero-based `(lower_index, upper_index)` into
    a sorted replicate list. It is public rather than private specifically so the test
    suite and a future auditor can assert the indices directly instead of inferring them
    from an interval, matching the auditability discipline `arena/adjudication.py:93-95`
    states for the correction columns. Its target state:
    - It calls `_require_resamples(resamples)` first, so the floor is enforced on every
      entry path.
    - `lower_index` is `math.floor(_LOWER_QUANTILE * (resamples + 1)) - 1`, clamped
      below at `0`.
    - `upper_index` is `math.ceil(_UPPER_QUANTILE * (resamples + 1)) - 1`, clamped above
      at `resamples - 1`.

    Document in its docstring or an adjacent comment the two properties this convention
    buys, because both are asserted in Task 2 and a future reader will otherwise
    "simplify" them away:
    - **Symmetry.** `lower_index == resamples - 1 - upper_index` at every admissible R.
      The current code has no such property: at R=10,000 it takes order statistic 251
      from the bottom and 9750 from the top.
    - **Coverage at or above nominal.** The interval spans
      `upper_index - lower_index + 1` of the `resamples` order statistics, which is at
      least `0.95 * resamples` at every admissible R. The `(resamples + 1)` denominator
      is the Efron-Tibshirani convention: with R replicates there are R+1 gaps between
      and outside them, so the alpha/2 quantile is the `(R+1) * alpha/2`-th order
      statistic. The current arithmetic uses `resamples` as the denominator and drops
      one index on the upper side, which is where the 94.99% comes from.

    Worked values to include in the comment so the arithmetic is checkable by eye:
    at R=10,000 the indices are `(249, 9750)`, spanning 9,502 of 10,000 replicates
    (95.02%); at R=500 they are `(11, 488)`, spanning 478 (95.6%); at R=200 they are
    `(4, 195)`, spanning 192 (96.0%); at R=40 they are `(0, 39)`, the full range.

    **Replace `_require_resamples` (lines 101-103)** so it rejects
    `resamples < MINIMUM_RESAMPLES` with the message
    `f"resample count must be at least {MINIMUM_RESAMPLES}"`. Keep it a module-private
    helper called from both `paired_bootstrap` and `paired_permutation`.

    **Replace the `lower=` and `upper=` arguments in the `BootstrapResult` construction
    (lines 152-158)** so both come from a single `percentile_indices(resamples)` call
    made once before the construction, indexing the already-sorted `deltas` list. Do not
    change `delta=`, `standard_error=` (which stays `statistics.pstdev(deltas)`) or
    `resamples=`. Do not touch the BCa-rejection comment at lines 144-151 other than to
    keep it adjacent and accurate; percentile remains the chosen flavour and BCa remains
    rejected for the reasons already recorded there.

    Explicitly out of scope: any change to `paired_permutation`'s p-value arithmetic, to

    quantity derived from it — MDD, sigma-hat, `expected_max_of_k`, `corrected_delta`,
    the practical-floor verdict — is numerically unchanged; only `ci_lower` and
